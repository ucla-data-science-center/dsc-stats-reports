Research Supported by Shared Investments

What DSC’s data purchases, platforms, and computing have made possible, and what they need to keep working

DSC buys data, runs platforms, and maintains computing that individual labs could not easily provide for themselves. These shared investments support identifiable research, and each needs ongoing data, access, and technical stewardship to keep working. Two of UCLA’s six 2024 Public Impact Research Award recipients led research that DSC supported (UCLA Newsroom).

Researchers own the scholarship; DSC supplied the documented data, access, or computing described below.

How the functions connect

DSC works with five kinds of research material. Only the first is a repository DSC runs; for the others DSC provides access, computing, capture, or code, and the material stays with its licensor, lab, or partner.

Material What DSC does Where the material lives Evidence
Repository datasets Operates UCLA Dataverse (campus sign-in, DOIs, preservation), where Library staff curate the legacy social science archive harvested into it UCLA Dataverse, run by DSC UCLA Dataverse; infrastructure page
Large or restricted data Acquired the L2 national voter file with a Library subject selector, scripted its ingest, set up the data use agreement and user management, and provides governed access Redivis, under the vendor license Voter file
Spatial data and imagery Provides UCLA’s access to LARIAC imagery and LiDAR and EagleView accounts, and GIS advice on using them Consortium and vendor platforms LARIAC; helicopter case
3D capture and models Scans, processes, and hosts 3D models for browser access pointcloud.ucla.edu, run by DSC; the objects and their stories belong to partners 3D case
Research software and workflows Staff and DataSquad students write and teach code with labs, and DSC runs the R server and deep learning machine the code runs on Lab and public code repositories; DSC servers Research computing; helicopter and deaths-in-custody cases

What ties the rows together is the archive’s working knowledge: how data is described, licensed, secured, and prepared so others can use it. Under any arrangement, someone has to be accountable for connecting these functions, setting standards across them, maintaining the infrastructure, and carrying that expertise forward.

Voter file and governed access (Redivis)

Research barrier National voter history arrives as thousands of state files, over 10 TB, too large to store and process locally and too inconsistent to analyze as delivered. The records contain personal information such as names and addresses, so the data has to be held securely, and its license requires governed access.
DSC investment Acquired the L2 national voter file with a Library subject selector. DSC’s Jamie Jamison scripted the data ingest for a doctoral curator, who made it analysis-ready as one national table, and set up the data use agreement and user management. DSC provides governed access through Redivis, a platform for restricted research data.
Documented use Redivis at UCLA: ~20TB of protected data, 50 registered users, and 150+ workflows (users and workflows, not projects). Research uses named publicly: election administration, gentrification, Latino voting, and a project linking voter records with health records.
Research outputs See the verified outputs below: the FORCE11 2024 presentation and an award-winning dissertation on U.S. election administration.
Evidence of the connection A 2024 conference presentation co-given by the platform’s founder and the UCLA curator; a paper using the L2 voter file that thanks the curator for access.
Coverage and limits Drawn from the presentation and the public work of the researchers it names. Not yet a census of Redivis projects.
Continuing requirement Data license renewal, platform subscription, curation of new releases, access administration, and onboarding for new researchers.

Research computing

Research barrier Some datasets exceed what a lab can host or process on its own machines.
DSC investment Hosts research servers and a deep learning machine, with staff and student support for processing and training.
Documented use Multi-year support for the Carceral Ecologies lab’s helicopter-surveillance analysis.
Research outputs One measured data-loading step fell from about 318 minutes to about 2, about 159 times as fast (claim #1). The lab’s research was later recognized by a 2024 UCLA Public Impact Research Award.
Evidence of the connection Case record and the researcher’s own account, shared with permission.
Coverage and limits One engagement shown; a second (machine-learning analysis of animal behavior) awaits the researcher’s confirmation. Job logs have not been compiled.
Continuing requirement Hardware maintenance, system administration, and staff who can support researchers on it.

UCLA Dataverse

Research barrier Researchers need a trusted place to deposit, cite, and share data, and UCLA’s legacy social science archive needed a modern home.
DSC investment Operates UCLA Dataverse with campus sign-in, DOI minting, and preservation, and harvested the legacy social science archive into it.
Documented use ~1,390 released datasets (February 2026), including about 1,014 legacy archive datasets, with download activity on the infrastructure evidence page.
Research outputs Publications that depositors linked to their datasets are listed as traceable publication links.
Evidence of the connection DOI relationships supplied by depositors.
Coverage and limits Depositors do not always link publications, and a linked paper is often the depositor’s own, so these links are not a measure of reuse.
Continuing requirement Platform operation, storage, curation, and DOI services.

LARIAC aerial imagery and LiDAR

Research barrier High-resolution aerial imagery and LiDAR for Los Angeles County is costly and licensed through a consortium of public agencies.
DSC investment UCLA is a member of the Los Angeles Region Imagery Acquisition Consortium (LARIAC) through the Library, and DSC is how the UCLA community gets the data: imagery, LiDAR, elevation models, and building footprints, free to UCLA researchers on request (DSC access guide). DSC also provisions EagleView imagery accounts.
Documented use 8 to 12 active EagleView users a year (2023 to 2026). Researchers from environment and sustainability, geography, and engineering, and campus Facilities for planning.
Research outputs A 2025 npj Natural Hazards study by UCLA authors built on 2020 LARIAC LiDAR (see below).
Evidence of the connection The paper states its LiDAR source; UCLA’s access to LARIAC runs through DSC.
Coverage and limits Papers rarely name how they obtained the data. Other UCLA papers use LARIAC-derived products that may circulate outside the license, so they are not counted.
Continuing requirement Consortium membership, data storage, access administration, and account provisioning.

GIS and geospatial support

UCLA cut its GIS librarian position in 2024. GIS requests continued.

Geography has consistently been DSC’s single largest workshop constituency: 64 of the 1,575 UCLA-affiliated workshop attendance records from 2020 to April 2024, more than any other department.

Before the position was eliminated in 2024, DSC’s GIS librarian handled 72 to 82 booked consultations a year (2021 to 2023) across Urban Planning, Public Affairs, Environment and Sustainability, and Digital Humanities. They also co-taught a graduate Public Health course, ran systemwide UC GIS workshops that reached 617 participants in 2023 alone, and managed key research datasets, including the LARIAC aerial imagery acquired through the Archive’s gift fund. That demand hasn’t gone away. DSC still fields GIS requests, just without the specialist capacity to meet them.

Nationally, 81 of 187 Carnegie R1 institutions (43%) maintain a dedicated GIS librarian, rising to 81% among the top 25 R&D-spending universities. UCLA has the highest research expenditure of any UC campus, yet is one of only two UC R1 campuses without one. The other, Santa Cruz, spends a fraction of what UCLA does on research. (UCSF also lacks one, but it’s a health-sciences-only campus with no undergraduates, not a fair comparison.) Lower-spending UC peers like Berkeley and San Diego both maintain dedicated GIS staff.

The campus mapping platform is a separate gap, a matter of ownership rather than consultation demand. UCLA’s ArcGIS Online platform, used across Geography, Luskin, Fielding, Digital Humanities, Political Science, and Social Data Science, has over 7,100 accounts and more than 3,000 active users in the past year, per an OARC governance report from December 2025. Without a designated administrator, it’s now running at over 200% of its licensed storage.

3D capture and Potree

Doug Daniels, DSC’s emerging technologies librarian, was named UCLA’s 2026 LAUC-LA Librarian of the Year.

The award, one of two given university-wide by LAUC-LA in a 15-nomination, peer-selected cycle, recognized partnerships across archaeology, digital humanities, medical sciences, materials science, and Egyptology, including the Refugee Material Culture Initiative (Research enabled) and a drone survey of Iceland’s Mosfell Valley with a doctoral student from the Cotsen Institute of Archaeology. A separate Lux Lab collaboration, a surgical probe holder built with an undergraduate student, is now in clinical use at Ronald Reagan UCLA Medical Center.

Potree, the spatial-visualization platform DSC runs alongside Dataverse, is what turns this work into something researchers can use rather than just store: 3D scans and point-cloud data become interactive models, explorable in a browser instead of sitting as a static file in an archive.

“Daniels’ work has strengthened partnerships across campus and throughout the greater Los Angeles community while helping students succeed with their individual research projects.” — Patrick Lavey, LAUC-LA Award Committee Chair, UCLA Newsroom

How outputs were selected

We reviewed each investment’s available records: project records, the public work of researchers named in DSC presentations, depositor links in UCLA Dataverse, and a full-text search of OpenAlex for LARIAC. An output is listed only when a public output exists and its connection to the investment is supported by an explicit citation, acknowledgment, or methods statement, or by a project record confirmed by the researcher, and when the researcher has agreed to be named. Citation counts and researcher prominence did not affect selection. Outputs still being confirmed, including work on machine-learning analysis of animal behavior and three further LARIAC papers, are not shown.
